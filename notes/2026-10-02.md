## 2. My view

I have no directional view against what is priced. My own test says I should
not expect one: over 132 meetings from 2011 to 2022, eve-of-meeting market
pricing scored a Brier of 0.077 against 0.355 for my reaction function, and
neither the model nor my GDP nowcast added information once pricing was
included. So the 15% the model gives a November hike, against 20% priced, is
not a reason to trade.

What the same test did show is a pattern in the market's own pricing. This note
sets out a rule to trade that pattern, written before the event so that I
cannot adjust it afterwards.

### The evidence

How often the RBA moved in the priced direction, by how much was priced the
day before the meeting (1-month OIS, 2011 to 2022):

| Priced on the eve | Meetings | RBA moved | Average result of fading the move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% to 25% | 25 | 0 (0%) | +3.3bp |
| 25% to 50% | 4 | 1 (25%) | too few to judge |
| 50% to 75% | 11 | 6 (55%) | about zero |
| 75% to 90% | 5 | 5 (100%) | too few to judge |
| 90% or more | 14 | 13 (93%) | not applicable; following it lost 3bp on average |

When a small chance of a move was priced, the move never came. That is
consistent with a premium for tail outcomes: someone pays a little over fair
value to be protected against a surprise.

When a move was 90% or more priced, the market was right 13 times in 14. That
is fair pricing, not an edge. Going with it earns about 2.5bp when right and
loses about 22bp when wrong (April 2015), which nets to slightly negative.

### What weakens the evidence

- 25 meetings is a small sample, and 21 of them were small priced cuts. Only 4
  were small priced hikes, which is the case I face now.
- Many of the 25 had only 2bp priced. OIS is quoted to the nearest basis point,
  so some of those are noise rather than a real premium. About 9 had 3.5bp or
  more priced.
- The research used OIS; I will trade cash rate futures, which are close but
  not identical.
- The test reads pricing the day before the meeting. It says nothing about
  pricing a month out, so it does not justify a position today.

## 3. Trade

**No position today.** The pattern is an eve-of-meeting result. Buying the
November contract now would be a bet on the 28 October CPI, on which I have no
view. I am committing to the rules below instead.

### Rule A: fade a small priced move

- **When I check:** the morning of Tuesday 3 November, using the settlement
  prices for Monday 2 November. The decision is at 2:30pm that day.
- **Signal:** the "As share of 25bp" figure for the 3 November meeting.
- **If a hike is priced between 5% and 25%:** buy the November 2026 cash rate
  futures contract (IBX2026).
- **If a cut is priced between 5% and 25%:** sell IBX2026.
- **Otherwise:** no trade, and I record that in the next note.
- **Entry:** the 2 November settlement price, committed and pushed before
  2:30pm on 3 November.
- **Exit:** the 3 November settlement price, closed the next morning. There is
  no discretion; this is a one-day trade around the decision.
- **Size:** contracts such that a 25bp move against me loses no more than
  $10,000. For a priced hike the two outcomes are a final settlement of 95.400
  on a hold (my target) and 95.175 on a hike (my stop).

| 10% | 95.378 | $499 | 20 | -$9,990 | +$1,110 |

| 25% | 95.344 | $416 | 24 | -$9,990 | +$3,330 |

The payoff is lopsided on purpose: I risk about $10,000 to make $1,100 to
$3,300. The rule only makes money if moves happen less often than priced, so
one loss wipes out four to nine wins.

### Rule B: when a move is 90% or more priced

No trade. The market has been right 93% of the time at that level, which is
what 90%-plus pricing should mean. Following it risks about 22bp to make about
2.5bp, and fading it is betting against a well-calibrated market.

### Review

- I apply Rule A at every meeting and report each result in the scorecard,
  including meetings where the signal did not fire.
- After two losses, or after eight trades, I stop and reassess whether the
  premium exists before trading it again.

### Before the meeting

Quarterly CPI is released on 28 October. Whatever it does to pricing, the rule
above is unchanged: only the pricing on the eve of the meeting matters.
